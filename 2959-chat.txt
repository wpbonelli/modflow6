● Pulled the full thread (OP by thomgraf1974, one question from aprovost-usgs,
  two clarifying replies). Here's my read.

  What's most likely going on

  Everything points to the near-boundary velocity / specific-discharge 
  reconstruction, not to the transport solver or the heterogeneity as a physical
  effect:

  - The GWF flow field is identical in both runs (only the GWT source location
    moved), so the difference is entirely in how the transport side uses the
    flows in the left column.
  - MF6 reconstructs a cell-centered velocity vector in each cell from face
    flows plus boundary flows. In a CHD cell this reconstruction is known to be 
    unreliable (it's called out in the NPF docs for SAVE_SPECIFIC_DISCHARGE:
    values "in constant-head cells and cells adjacent to constant-head cells may
    be inaccurate"). The CHD boundary flow has to be smeared back onto faces,
    and the least-squares/averaging step degrades badly when neighboring K
    differs.
  - That reconstructed velocity is exactly what drives mechanical dispersion (D
    ≈ αL·|v|, αT·|v|) in the Eulerian GWT, or the interpolated particle velocity
    if this is a random-walk / MODPATH-style run (the "number of particles"
    comment suggests it might be). Either way, a corrupted |v| and its gradient
    in the boundary column mis-scales spreading. Provost's observation that the
    bad result "looks like much lower dispersion" is consistent with the
    boundary-cell |v| being underestimated.

  So: put the constant-concentration source in the CHD column and the plume
  forms in the one place where the velocity is least trustworthy → unphysical.
  Move it one column in and the source sits in an interior cell with a clean
  velocity → looks fine. Make the domain homogeneous and the boundary-cell
  reconstruction collapses to the uniform regional flow → the co-located case
  also works.

  Why co-locating CNC + CHD makes it worse

  Beyond the bad velocity, two things stack on top when the cells coincide:

  1. SSM skips constant-concentration cells (ibound <= 0). When the source CHD
     cells are also CNC, the CHD inflow mixing term is not assembled there — the
     mass instead enters only as the CNC "constant-concentration flow." That's
     mathematically fine for the pinned cell, but it means the boundary inflow
     no longer participates in the local advective balance in the column,
     changing how the pinned value couples to column 2.
  2. Cross-dispersion at a pinned boundary node. With the standard (non-XT3D)
     dispersion formulation, cross terms are lagged/RHS-treated. A sharp fixed
     concentration gradient sitting on top of a distorted velocity field at the
     boundary is a recipe for over/undershoot and blotchy patterns — "weird and
     unphysical."

  Why a 1% K change (9.9 vs 10) is enough

  Because it's not a physical-heterogeneity effect — it's the reconstruction
  switching from "trivially exact by symmetry" to "approximate." Any K contrast
  touching the boundary source cells tilts the reconstructed velocity vector
  (adds a spurious transverse component, changes |v|), and that immediately
  perturbs the dispersion coefficients / particle displacements right where the
  source is. The magnitude of the contrast barely matters; its presence does.

  Is it a bug?

  I'd call it a known accuracy limitation rather than a defect, but it's worth
  having them post the input files so someone can check the calc_spdis path for
  CHD cells adjacent to K contrasts, and confirm whether XT3D changes it. If
  specific discharge in those cells is visibly wrong, that's the smoking gun.

  What to suggest to the user

  - Keep the source one cell inside the CHD boundary — their working
    configuration. This is generally recommended practice; MF6 examples
    typically place the solute inlet just inside the flow boundary for exactly
    this reason.
  - Turn on SAVE_SPECIFIC_DISCHARGE and plot the q-vectors in the left column
    for the 9.9 vs 10.0 cases — this should directly show the artifact.
  - Try XT3D (in NPF, and it propagates to dispersion) — better velocity and
    cross-dispersion reconstruction near the heterogeneity; likely reduces or
    removes the discrepancy.
  - As an alternative to CNC at the boundary, impose the inlet concentration via
    CHD AUXILIARY + SSM so the mass enters with the water rather than being
    pinned in the worst-velocity cell.
  - Check the GWT mass budget (LST) for both configs — an oversized or
    oscillating CONSTANT CONC term in the co-located case confirms the CNC is
    fighting a bad local flow field.
  - Refine the grid near the boundary; if it's reconstruction sensitivity,
    refinement should shrink it.
  - Clarify GWT (finite-volume) vs a particle method — the "particles" wording
    is ambiguous and the exact fix differs (XT3D/dispersion vs
    velocity-interpolation scheme near boundaries).
